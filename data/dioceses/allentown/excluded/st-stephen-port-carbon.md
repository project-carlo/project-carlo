## Why it is excluded

Closed as a parish church in 2020, then sold by the diocese. AD Today, the diocese's news
site, reported on 24 October 2022 that "St. Stephen Parish was merged into St. Clare of
Assisi Parish, Saint Clair, in 2020 because of declining attendance and financial
struggles", and that the diocese had agreed "to sell the former Saint Stephen Church in Port
Carbon to the Saint Stephen Society", a group of former parishioners. St. Clare of Assisi
(`st-clare-st-clair-holy-cross-new-philadelphia`) took over St. Stephen's assets and
liabilities. Its current bulletin (11 October 2026) lists Masses at St. Clare and Holy Cross
only. The St. Clare pages that have text (page 1 of the 11 October 2026 issue and of the 4
January and 1 February 2026 issues) don't mention St. Stephen; pages 2–3 use an embedded font
that extracts as garbage, so they weren't searched.

The building is not entirely without Mass: the same article says the Society "expects to
sponsor the celebration of two Masses per year", on 26 December and 7 February. That's two
Masses a year in a privately owned former church, announced in no bulletin the pipeline
reads. I filed it as `closed` (it closed as a parish church, and the diocese no longer
owns it) rather than `no-bulletin`. Whether those two Masses still happen wasn't checked.

The closure date: WNEP reported the merger on 8 December 2019, and a search summary of that
article gives 12 January 2020 as the effective date. The article itself couldn't be read
from the sandbox, so `last_mass` is the year AD Today gives, `2020`.

## What would change this

If the Saint Stephen Society's Masses turn out to be regular and announced somewhere
readable (for example in the St. Clare bulletin), this could become a church row under
St. Clare with `reason` reconsidered. To date the closure exactly, read the WNEP article
of 8 December 2019 in a normal browser, or the St. Clare/St. Stephen bulletins of January
2020.
