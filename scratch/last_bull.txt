AMDG, 
Fr. Tom
Dear Parishioners,
It’s Labor Day weekend and summer vacation is over! School starts on Tuesday!
Where, oh where, did the summertime go? Summer seem s to fly by faster than
any other season. Every year, I’m left with a long list of things I wanted to do
but never got around to. Ready or not, Labor Day is here. 
Labor Day became a federal holiday in 1894 when Pre sident Grover Cleveland
(a former mayor of Buffalo) signed legislation to h onor the contributions and
achievements of American workers. 
PAGE 2 TWENTY-THIRD SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME
So, as we acknowledge the unofficial end of summer and the official beginning of a new school year, le t us
remember and celebrate the dignity of work. Work is a gift from God. In our labor, we participate with God in
continuing the creation of the world.
“...God entrusted the earth and its resources to
the common stewardship of mankind...”
The Catholic Church has a long history of promoting the dignity of work. The 
Catechism states, “In the beginning, God entrusted the earth and its resources 
to the common stewardship of mankind to take care o f them, master them by labor, and enjoy their fruit s”
(CCC, 2402). Thus, work is more than just a way to make a living. It is a participation with God as co -creators
to transform the world.
Many popes have written extensively on the topic of  labor. They have focused on the rights and
responsibilities of workers. Workers always take pr iority over production and profit. Workers deserve fair
compensation for the legitimate fruits of their lab or. Workers have a right to productive work, and to  a safe
working environment. 
I hope you had a wonderful and relaxing summer. May  the fall season, with its beautiful array of color s,
bring you joy and peace. 
Lord on this Labor Day,
we thank You for the blessing of work.
We ask for strength to complete each day.
We ask for rest when we are weary.
We ask Your guidance
for everyone seeking employment,
and we ask that
You be with those whose faces
we might never see
but who work tirelessly each day
for the good of us all.
Amen.

St. Peter Claver was a Spanish Jesuit missionary
who served ensalved Africans in Cartagena,
Colombia. Calling himself “the slave of the
Negroes forever,” he brought food, medicine,
faith instruction, and compassion to those
arriving by ship. He baptized and catechized
many and is honored as the patron saint of
African Americans and enslaved people.
 
St. Peter Claver died on September 8, 1654. He
was canonized with his friend St. Alphonsus
Rodriguez in 1888 by Pope Leo XIII. ©LPi

The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches us tha t prayer is a lifting up of our
hearts and minds to God. The Christian family is th e first place of education in
prayer. Based on the sacrament of marriage, the fam ily is the “domestic Church”
where God’s children learn to pray “as the Church” and to persevere in prayer. For
young children in particular, daily family prayer i s the first witness of the Church’s
living memory as awakened patiently by the Holy Spirit (CCC 2685). 
WHAT IS PRAYER?
When we think of prayer, our first thoughts may tur n to attending Sunday Mass,
but what about the rest of the week? Here are a few suggestions:
– As a family, carve out a corner of a room for a s mall prayer table or bench. Cover it with a tablecl oth
and then place prayer-related items on it: a Children’s Bible, crucifix, holy water, rosary, a statue of Our
Lady or the Sacred Heart, a flameless candle to remind us of God’s presence. Add seasonal decor, too! 
– Pray the rosary (or just one decade of the rosary) near your prayer table each evening.
– Encourage your children to say a simple prayer ea ch time they pass the prayer table. The prayer
might be, “Jesus, I trust in You!” or “Jesus, thank you for this day!”
– Learn about Saints whose feast days fall in the c urrent month. Create items or artwork that remind
you of the saints and add them to your prayer table . Ask the saints to help you live the virtues they
demonstrated in their own lives. Feast days for Sept ember can be found on USCCB.org > Prayer &
Worship > Daily Readings Calendar.
